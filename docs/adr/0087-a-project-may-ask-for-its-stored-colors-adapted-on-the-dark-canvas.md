# A project may ask for its stored colors adapted on the dark canvas

ADR 0033 draws every stored color exactly as stored in both themes. On the dark canvas that makes a pale note, a pink trigger or a light fill a package stored glare as a white block among dark ones, which is what A5 found on the user's model. ADR 0033 rejected a dark variant beside each stored color, since it doubles every appearance, command and tool, and it rejected moving built-in colors to mid-tones. This ADR supersedes ADR 0033's "drawn exactly as stored in the light theme and the dark theme alike" for the projects and views that ask for it, and only on the dark canvas.

## Decision

- **A drawing default, `storedColorsInDark`, is `"as-stored"` or `"adapted"`** (ADR 0086). Every preset has `"as-stored"`, so nothing changes for a project that does not ask. A project may set it, and a view may set it again for itself, through `set-drawing-defaults`, the Drawing panel and Claude's `set_drawing`, as any other drawing default.
- **Adapted means a stored color's lightness is turned for the dark theme, its hue and chroma kept.** In CIELAB, the lightness L\* of a fill becomes `100 − L*`, with a and b kept and the result brought back into sRGB by lowering chroma until it fits. Strokes, a tint and the colors of notes and free arrows are turned the same way. Text on a shape keeps taking the color that reads on the fill it is drawn on, and a stroke that falls under 3:1 against its adapted fill is outlined as ADR 0033's W49 clarification says. Turning twice gives back the stored color, so the light theme and the dark theme never drift apart.
- **Adapting only draws.** No command, file or tool result changes a stored color. The light theme always draws them as stored. Claude's tool results and the inspector show the stored color, and say what the dark canvas draws when that differs.
- **The same module resolves it for everyone.** `core/src/drawing.ts` resolves the setting with the other drawing defaults; one pure function in `core` turns a color, so the canvas, the read-only page, the SVG and PDF exports and `capture_view` draw the same adapted colors in the dark theme. The inspector's color picker, on the dark canvas with adapted colors, shows the stored color with the color it is drawn in beside it, and picking compares with what the shape would show, as ADR 0033's A2 clarification says.
- **Tests hold it:** turning twice is the identity within one step of each channel (a property test), adapted fills keep 3:1 for marks against the dark canvas where the stored fill kept 3:1 against the light one, built-in colors, which already have a dark shade, are left as they are, and the frame budget holds, since a theme switch already draws again the tiles whose colors change.

## Considered

- **Adapted by default:** rejected by the user. A project's colors stay what its author picked unless it asks.
- **A dark variant beside each stored color:** still rejected, for ADR 0033's reasons.
- **Inverting the color whole:** rejected. It turns the hue, so a red trigger would draw cyan.

## Clarifications

<!-- Decided by Claude on 2026-11-02 under the user's standing delegation of design decisions, building A5.8. -->

- 2026-11-02, A5.8: **where the setting lives.** In a group of its own, `colors`, as `drawing.colors.storedColorsInDark`, so that the checks, the layering, the Drawing panel and Claude's `get_drawing` and `set_drawing` take it as they take every other value (`DRAWING_GROUPS`, `DRAWING_VALUES`). Every preset, and `DRAWING_BEFORE_A5`, has `"as-stored"`. The core's `storedColorsIn(drawing, theme)` gives how a theme draws stored colors: `"adapted"` only on the dark canvas of a drawing that asks for it.
- 2026-11-02, A5.8: **the one function.** `adaptedForDark` in `core/src/colors.ts`, beside `labOf`: L\* becomes 100 − L\*, a and b are kept, and where that falls outside sRGB a and b are scaled down together, the hue kept, to the most that fits (by halving). Of the eight ways to round the result to `#RRGGBB`, it writes the one whose own turn comes nearest the color, which halves the error of turning twice. It is memoized (4,096 colors), reads either case, writes capitals, and gives back the stored spelling of a color that turns into itself.
- 2026-11-02, A5.8: **the bound turning twice holds.** It gives the color back wherever its turn kept its chroma: over every seventh color of the sRGB cube (2,396,746 colors), the 1,154,295 whose turn keeps its chroma came back exactly in 59.0 %, within one step of each channel in 40.7 %, within two in 0.26 % and within three in 43 colors, never more, and always within ΔE 1.46, under what a person notices. One step everywhere cannot be held: the low channel of a saturated color is hardly determined by its CIELAB values once the others are rounded. The property test holds three steps and ΔE 1.5 (`core/src/adapted-colors.test.ts`). A color whose turn had to lose chroma, such as a pale yellow whose dark twin cannot be as colorful, comes back with its lightness, within 1 L\*, and that lower chroma. Since nothing ever stores an adapted color, the light theme and the dark theme still never drift apart: the light theme draws the stored color, the dark one its turn.
- 2026-11-02, A5.8: **the contrast bound.** L\* alone fixes a color's luminance, so contrast follows from it; but WCAG's ratio is not symmetric about L\* 50 and the dark canvas, #16191E, is not black. A stored color with 3:1 against the light canvas (L\* 61.7 or less) has at least 2.5:1 against the dark one after turning (2.54:1 at worst); one with 3.5:1 keeps 3:1, and one with 4.5:1 keeps 3.9:1. The property test holds these three. Where a mark falls under 3:1, what already guards stored colors applies to the colors drawn: a block under 3:1 against either canvas is outlined, W49 outlines a shape whose container's stroke is under 3:1 against its fill, and a built-in fill keeps its light value beside a stroke drawn under 3:1 on the dark canvas. A pivot of 104.3 − L\* would carry 3:1 to 3:1 exactly, at the cost of turning near-black colors beyond white; it is not built, since this ADR says 100 − L\*, and is left to the user.
- 2026-11-02, A5.8: **which colors are adapted.** Every color a trait takes from anything but a built-in definition itself: an element's or a relation's own, a project appearance, even one given to a built-in definition, and the colors of a project's own packages, as well as a stored image tint and the own colors of notes and free arrows. A trait that comes from a built-in definition keeps its dark shade, or its light value where it has none (`componentDarkColorsOf`, `relationTypeDarkStrokeOf`, `darkColorsOf` and `relationDarkStrokeOf` take the setting). Colors the theme gives, notes and free arrows without their own included, are never adapted. Overlays' colors are not either: their palettes are chosen to read on both canvases and the legend shows them as drawn (ADR 0080); the relation types' legend shows each type's stroke as the dark canvas draws it.
- 2026-11-02, A5.8: **how it is drawn.** The scene does it once, so that the canvas, the read-only page, the SVG and PDF and `capture_view`, which all draw the scene, draw the same: an element's `dark` and a line's `darkStroke` carry the adapted colors, a note or an arrow with colors of its own carries `adapted`, which `paint.ts` passes to the core's `noteColorsOf` and `arrowColorOf`, and a stored tint carries `darkTint`. A tile with any of them follows the theme, and a note's text is tinted again, reading on the fill drawn. The canvas draws a tile's pictures again for the theme when one of its silhouettes has a `darkTint`, within its four drawings a frame, so such a silhouette may show its stored tint for a few frames after a switch. Turning the setting on or off changes the drawing defaults, so the view is laid out afresh as for any drawing default; its shapes differ only in their colors, and their tiles are restyled in place.
- 2026-11-02, A5.8: **the inspector.** It shows the stored color, which is what a pick changes, and, on the dark canvas of a view that adapts stored colors, "Drawn #… on the dark canvas" with a swatch beside it where the two differ; built-in colors show their dark shade, as before. Picks are compared as ADR 0033's A2 says, with what the shape would show without a color of its own, in stored colors: picking the stored color again records nothing, and picking the inherited one takes the shape's own back. The hints under a color measure the dark canvas with the colors it draws. The inspector reads the open view's setting.
- 2026-11-02, A5.8: **Claude.** `set_drawing`'s description says what the setting does and to set it only when the person asks. The answers of the tools that store colors measure the dark canvas with the colors it draws, naming the drawn color in a warning, and add, for each stored color it draws otherwise, "On the dark canvas the fill #… of "API" is drawn #…: the drawing defaults adapt stored colors there (colors.storedColorsInDark). The color stored stays #…." `get_item` adds `darkCanvas`, the colors drawn for those of the item's own appearance, beside `appearance`, which keeps the stored ones; `preview_face` and `set_face` measure faces on the dark canvas with the shape's adapted colors. They read the project's setting, and a free shape's tools the view's.
- 2026-11-02, A5.8: **the frame budget** was not measured with the benchmark, which needs a browser this build could not open. A switch draws again only the tiles whose colors change (`renderer.theme.test.ts`), a stored color that turns into itself makes its tile no different from today, and each color's turn is looked up once.
