# Joints move what they carry, and motion plays in the physical-space panel

M9 draws a solid inside its shape and M10 places elements in physical space with poses, but nothing moves: each pose stands on its own, and the Physical AI package's joints carry a kind, limits and an axis that nothing reads. A robot arm shows where its links are, not how they move.

## Decision

- **A joint's position is data.** The built-in Joint (`physical-ai.joint`) gains a number attribute, `position`: an angle in degrees for a joint that turns, a distance in meters for one that slides, 0 by default. Its `kind`, `axis` and limits, which it already has, say how it moves. A position outside the limits is pointed out, as every limit only warns (CLAUDE.md), and drawn clamped.
- **Joints move what they carry: forward kinematics in the core.** The chain is read from the model: a joint joins the link it hangs from and the link it carries, through the package's relations, and the carried link is posed in the joint's frame (M10's `Pose.in`). The pose of anything a joint carries, at any depth, is the joint's pose, turned about its axis by its position, or slid along it, composed with its own. One pure module, `core/src/space/kinematics.ts`, works it out, so the panel, Claude and the exports agree; a cycle of joints is refused, as containment's are. Elements without joints are posed as they are now.
- **Motion comes from four places, and none of them but the last changes the project:**
  1. **By hand:** the physical-space panel shows a slider for each joint of what it shows, and dragging moves the chain live. "Keep these positions" stores them as one action of the person's.
  2. **Tours and scenarios:** a step may give joints positions, as it gives a camera; between steps they glide over the step's time (ADR 0084's glides), so a tour plays the arm picking a part.
  3. **Simulation:** a programmable component's `simulate` (ADR 0085) may answer joints' positions each tick, and the panel follows the run.
  4. **Claude:** reads the chain with `get_kinematics` and sets positions with a command, as the person does.
- **Playing it.** The panel draws the moving chain at the 60 frames per second target with 200 joints and the M10 triangle budget, working the poses out in its worker. Reduced motion jumps to each step's positions. Relations attached to a moving part follow it, as they follow parts while turning (M9).
- **What it is not:** no gravity, no masses, no contact and no collision. A physics engine and a reach envelope stay possible later, each with an ADR of its own; the user chose kinematics first.

## Considered

- **Positions only in poses, without joints:** rejected. Moving a shoulder would mean moving each link by hand, and limits could not be checked.
- **Animations stored in the solid (glTF animations):** kept as M9 has them, played when asked; they are a file's own clip, not the model's joints, and they cannot follow a scenario or a simulation.
- **A physics engine now (Rapier in WebAssembly):** left for later by the user's choice, as heavier work and heavier for the laptop.

## Clarifications

- 2026-11-02 (M15.0, the seam, in the main session):
  - **The chain.** An element's parent in the chain is what it is attached to, through `physical-ai.is-attached-to` or a relation type derived from it (the robot-arm sample's "is mounted on" is one), when that is in physical space; otherwise what it is posed or anchored in. The first attachment an element starts counts. An attachment that closes a cycle is not followed, and opening warns (`attachment-cycle`).
  - **The motion.** Each element's matrix is the product, from the top down, of the motions of the joints above it, each worked out where that joint stands at rest, applied to where the element is at rest: a joint turns what it carries, not its own body. A joint's axis is read in its own frame, which for a joint anchored to a part of a solid is the solid's axes (glTF's, +Y up); its pivot is its origin, which for an anchored part is the part's point. `revolute` and `prismatic` positions are drawn within the limits, `continuous` turns without end, `fixed` and a joint without a readable axis move nothing. A position past its limits is warned about on opening (`joint-out-of-limits`).
  - **Where it lives.** `core/src/space/kinematics.ts`; `spaceOf(project, view, root, positions)` takes positions over the joints' own, as sliders and tours give them; `Space.motions` holds each moved element's motion, which the panel applies to an anchored part's node of its holder's solid, since a solid's parts are flat in the files seen so far (the robot-arm sample's are). `poseIn` measures from where things are once moved.
  - **Tours** give positions in a step's view state, `space.joints`, by joint id. Scenario steps take none in the seam; M15.2 plays them through the tour's stage and simulation, and may add them to steps with a clarification here.
  - **The sample's axes** are written as a robot's description would write them, with +Z up, while its solid is +Y up: M15.3 writes them in the solid's axes.
- 2026-11-02 (M15.1, the panel, by a builder):
  - **The joints' sliders** are a section of the panel under its ways of reading, "N joints", open at first and closed from its heading; it scrolls within itself past a few rows, so that 200 joints leave the frames their room. A slider for each joint among what the panel shows that moves (of the Joint or a component derived from it, neither `fixed` nor without a readable axis), in the order the space reaches them, named by the joint. Its range is its limits; a joint that turns without limits, or a `continuous` one, goes from −180° to 180°, and one that slides without limits from −1 m to 1 m; steps of 1° or 1 mm; its value is said as "40°" or as a length. Native range inputs, so the arrow keys, Page Up and Down, Home and End move them.
  - **Dragging stores nothing:** the panel keeps the positions given while it is open and hands them to `spaceOf`, so that what joints carry, the relations drawn in space, markers, names, framing and picking all follow. "Keep these positions" sends the positions that differ from the stored ones as one `batch` of `update-element` changes to `position`, labeled "Keep the joints' positions", one action of the person's that one undo takes back, and then lets them go; "Back to stored positions" lets them go, gliding back over 240 ms on the panel's frames, or at once while motion is reduced (ADR 0076). Nothing else in the panel tweens joints. The read-only page has the sliders, which only read, and no Keep.
  - **The worker** takes each moved entry's motion (`SpaceDrawnEntry.motion`, the core's `Space.motions`) and, on an entry drawing a solid, `jointed`: set by the panel when one of its anchored parts is a joint that moves or is moved. A jointed solid is drawn piece by piece from its pieces' own geometry, not merged (ADR 0076's batches), so that each part turns on its own; being jointed is part of what a scene is made of, so it is set by what the model holds, not by the positions, and the scene is not made again as a slider crosses 0. Each anchored part of a jointed holder turns its node, and every node it holds that no element stands for, by `holder⁻¹ · motion · holderMotion⁻¹ · holder`, in the solid's coordinates, after the exploded view's offset; a node that no anchored part holds stays with its solid, as it takes its solid's element's state. The names' points and framing read the same turn.
  - **The frame budget with 200 joints** (recorded, not held): in Node, on a chain of 200 joints and 200 links posed in World (401 entries, 399 moved), every joint moved at once, each move takes 6.1 ms at the median and 16.7 ms at the 95th percentile on the page (`spaceOf` with positions, the joints shown, the worker's entries), and 0.6 and 2.5 ms in the worker's layout; React's rendering of the panel, the states and the GPU's frame come on top and were not measured in a browser. The page's share is mostly `spaceOf` walking the whole space again for each move; a move that keeps the rest placement and composes only the motions would cut it, if a browser run shows the target missed.
  - **The sample's axes,** written in the solid's axes (+Y up, its front toward +Z, as `packages/server/scripts/robot-arm-solid.ts` builds the arm's frames): joint 1 `0 1 0` (the base turns about the vertical), joints 2, 3 and 5 `1 0 0` (the shoulder, the elbow and the wrist bend in the arm's plane, x = 0, about the horizontal across it; a positive position leans the arm forward, toward the bins), joints 4 and 6 `0 0 1` (the forearm and the tool roll along the forearm, which reaches toward +Z). They were `0 0 1`, `0 1 0`, `0 1 0`, `1 0 0`, `0 1 0` and `1 0 0`, a description with +Z up.
  - **Left for the main session** (see the report): a joint's pivot is its anchored part's point, which for the sample is a vertex on the joint's surface chosen to face the camera, not on its axis, so a turned link swings a few centimeters off its joint (6.5 cm off the shoulder's axis); and the solid's camera, which no element stands for since ADR 0076 posed the wrist camera, stays where it is while the wrist moves.

- 2026-11-02 (M15.2, part of it, by a builder; M15.2's tour editor and M15.3 are left):
  - **Origins.** `SolidPart.origin` is the node's world translation at rest, rounded as points are; `describeSolid` writes it for every named part and `define-solid` accepts it. A definition without it turns about the part's point, as before. In the sample every part's origin is its joint's frame, on the axis, so a link no longer swings off its joint: with joint 1 at 57°, every part above the base, joint 2's cylinder and the wrist camera included, turns by 57° about the vertical through joint 1's origin and the base does not (tested in `robot-arm.test.ts`, and on the worker's layout headless). The swing the user saw came from the pivot on joint 1's surface, 9 cm off the axis.
  - **The wrist camera** is anchored to the solid's `camera` part; its pose is gone, and the structure view keeps no place of its own for it.
  - **The workcell's solids.** The table, a bin and the workpiece are solids of the sample's package (`robot-arm.work-table`, `robot-arm.parts-bin`, `robot-arm.machined-block`), made of boxes by `packages/server/scripts/workcell-solids.ts` and written with the arm's by the `robot-arm` script, CC0-1.0; the table's and the bin's components show them as icons, and the workpiece has a component of its own, `robot-arm.workpiece`, and rests on bin A's floor. The workcell view now takes the arm's chain in (`physical-ai.is-attached-to` in its scope, `physical-ai.is-part-of` among its nesting types), drawn on the arm's picture, since a panel moves only the anchored parts its view shows.
  - **Joints' positions are the panel's state.** `ViewSpace.joints` holds the positions the panel shows over the joints' own: the sliders write them there, a tour's step and an address set them, "Keep these positions" stores and clears them, "Back to stored positions" glides them away. So Copy address and Take the canvas carry them. In an address they are `joints:<id>=<position>,…`, by id in order; a view state gives at most 200, each a number from −100,000 to 100,000, and names that are not joints are pointed out (`viewStateProblems`).
  - **Gliding between steps.** A tour's step that keeps showing the same element glides its joints from the step before to its own, or to their stored positions when it gives none, on the tour's ease, over 250 ms plus 150 ms for each 30° the farthest joint turns, or each 10 cm it slides, at most 1,200 ms (longer than ADR 0084's 800 ms, since a chain travels farther than a shape and is watched moving); it jumps while motion is reduced or the step always cuts, and an address puts them there at once. A slider moved meanwhile stops the glide where it is.
  - **Scenario playback** takes no joints of its own: a scenario played by a tour's step plays with the step's positions, and scenario playback alone leaves the panel's positions as they are.
  - **Simulation.** `simulate` may answer `joints`, an object of positions by joint id among what it may mark, at most 200, each a finite number (anything else drops the answer as the code's fault); a position past a joint's limits is drawn at the limit, as every limit only warns. The panel shows the runs' ticks' positions under its own state's, a run opened later over one opened before.
  - **The tour "Pick a part"** reaches over bin A, grips, lifts, crosses to bin B, places and goes back to rest in the workcell view, positions worked out so that the gripper reaches the part pointing down. The part itself stays drawn in bin A: a tour moves joints, not poses.
- 2026-11-02 (M15.2's tour editor and M15.3, by a builder):
  - **"Use the joints' positions here"** is a step's button in the tours panel, beside Take the canvas. It sets only the step's `space.joints`: every joint the physical-space panel of the step's view shows (at most 200), each at the position drawn there, the panel's own (a slider, a tour's step, an address) over a simulation's over the stored one. Every shown joint is written, not only those that differ from their stored positions, so that the step keeps showing the arm as it was taken even if stored positions change later. It is disabled, saying why in its description, while the step does not show the panel ("take the canvas with the panel open first"), while the panel of the step's view is closed, or while it shows no joints. `tours/step-joints.ts` works it out.
  - **Scenarios take no joints of their own**, as the clarification of M15.2's first part said: the scenario panel sets none, and the scenario tools take none; a scenario played by a tour's step plays with the step's positions. The spec's "the scenario panel setting them" and "scenarios' tools taking them" are met by the tour's step, not by scenario steps.
  - **`get_kinematics`** reads the chain of a robot, or of any element (what it contains at any depth, with everything that hangs from it), or of the whole model (every tree that holds a joint): depth first from the top, each element with what it hangs from and its depth, and each joint with its kind, its axis as written and as a unit vector, its limits, its stored position and the one it is drawn at when that differs, its unit, whether it moves and why not (fixed, a kind that does not move, an axis it cannot read), what it carries directly and how many elements in all; and the chain's warnings (`joint-out-of-limits`, `attachment-cycle`) from the core. An element hangs from what it is attached to, or else from what it is posed in, as the core reads the chain; elements anchored to a solid's parts without an attachment are not listed. 500 elements a call, paged.
  - **`set_joint_positions`**, not the attribute tool: a small tool that takes positions by joint id (at most 200, each from −100,000 to 100,000), refuses an element that is not there or is no joint, and sends one `batch` of `update-element` changes to `position`, labeled `Set the position of "<joint>"` or `Set the positions of N joints`, one action of Claude's that one undo takes back. A position past the limits is stored and warned about ("drawn at 120"), and a joint that moves nothing is warned about; `update_element` keeps working for the same attribute, without those checks.
  - **Claude's view states** take `space.joints` (`SPACE_PART`), so `get_view_address`, `open_app` and the tour tools carry joints' positions; the core checks them as it checks an address's. `show_space` takes none: its guidance has no joints, and showing positions without storing them goes through an address, `open_app` or a tour's step. `list_solid_parts` gives each part's `origin` when its solid has one.
