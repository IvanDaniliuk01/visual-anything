# The format panel edits a shape's style, text and arrangement, as draw.io's does

The inspector already lets a person change a selected element's shape kind, fill and stroke, a relation's stroke, width and dash, the stacking order, and, on the canvas, sizes, alignment and the grid. Everything else about how one shape looks comes only from its component or from drawing defaults (ADR 0086), and sizes and positions have no fields. draw.io shows all of it for the selection in three tabs. The user wants the same.

## Decision

- **The inspector's appearance for a selection becomes the format panel, with three tabs: Style, Text and Arrange.** It shows for one or several selected shapes, relations, notes and free arrows. A value the selection does not share shows as mixed, and setting it sets it on every selected item in one action.
- **Style and Text are appearance, stored as particularities of the element or relation** (ADR 0034's order: own appearance, component, project appearance, Essence; then drawing defaults). They draw the same in every view, as every appearance does. Styling one shape in one view only stays rejected (ADR 0034). The panel's scope choice, "Only this element" or "Every <component>", stays, and "Set as the project's default" writes a project appearance.
- **New appearance traits**, each optional, each undefined meaning "as the component, then drawing defaults":
  - **Style:** `borderWidth` and `dash` on shapes (as relations have), `cornerRadius`, `opacity` (0.2 to 1), `shadow` (`none` or `soft`), and `gradient` (a second stored color and a direction, top to bottom or left to right). Stored colors stay `#RRGGBB` (ADR 0033).
  - **Text:** `font` (the fonts drawing defaults allow), `fontSize`, `bold`, `italic`, `underline` (the whole name; emphasis inside text stays Markdown, ADR 0082), `textAlign` (left, center, right), `textPlacement` (exists: top, middle, bottom), `labelPosition` (inside, or a tag above, below, left or right, which generalizes name tags), `textColor`, `labelFill` and `labelStroke` for a label's back, `wrap`, and `padding`. A text color that reads under 4.5:1 on its fill is pointed out, never refused.
- **Arrange is the view's**, since it is layout: numeric width, height (with "Keep proportions") and position for the selection, stored as today in the view's shape positions and pinning the shape; stacking order (exists); align and distribute (exists, ADR 0074); "Same size" (exists); the container a shape sits in (draw.io's "Group"); pin and unpin (draw.io's "Lock"); and, new, `rotation` in degrees and `flip` (horizontal, vertical) of a shape in a view. A rotated shape draws its outline, fill and image rotated and keeps its name level; lines attach to its rotated outline; layout and routing use its rotated bounds. Rotation and flip do not apply to containers drawn as regions or to rows.
- **Copy appearance and paste appearance** copy every Style and Text trait of one item and set them on the selection in one action; "Copy size" is the existing "Same size".
- **A palette of swatches** comes first in every color field: twelve colors chosen to pass the contrast tests on both canvases, held in core beside the built-in colors, then the project's colors and recent ones (A2).
- **Changed by commands, as today:** `update-element` and `update-relation` take the new traits, `set-component-appearance` and project appearances take them too, and `set-positions` takes `rotation` and `flip`. Every change is undoable and records its author. Claude reads and sets them through its existing appearance tools, which list the new traits; `capture_view` checks the result.
- **Everything that draws reads them:** the canvas, far-out drawing, the read-only page, SVG and PDF, and comparisons. The frame budget at 10,000 elements holds with every trait set on every shape (shadows and gradients drawn once per tile, as A5's shadows are), and the contrast tests cover the palette.

## Considered

- **Per-view styling, as draw.io's per-cell styles:** rejected again, for ADR 0034's reasons: a model shown in several views would drift, and Claude would have to repeat every change in every view.
- **A free-form style string, as draw.io's:** rejected. Named, typed traits are validated on open, diffed by versions and merged (ADR 0072).
- **Glass and Sketch:** left out. They are decoration the Clean look does not want; a preset may add them later.

## Clarifications

- 2026-11-02 (A6.0, the seam, in the main session):
  - **The traits' names and ranges.** An appearance gains `borderWidth` (0 to 16, 0 drawing no border), `dash`, `cornerRadius` (0 to 64, rounding rectangles of either kind only), `opacity` (0.2 to 1, the text staying whole), `shadow` (`none` or `soft`), `gradient` (`{ to, direction: "down" | "right" }`, or `none` to take an inherited one away) and `text`, a `TextStyle` of `font` (`roboto`, `system` or `mono`), `size` (8 to 48), `bold`, `italic`, `underline`, `align`, `position` (`inside`, `above`, `below`, `left`, `right`), `color`, `background`, `border`, `wrap` and `padding` (0 to 48). Each part of `text` is inherited on its own, as each part of a detail is, and the trait paths name them `text.<part>`. `textPlacement` stays a trait of its own, for files written before.
  - **What resolves to what.** A resolved appearance always has `dash` (Essence's `solid`), `opacity` (1) and `text` (Essence's `{}`); `borderWidth`, `cornerRadius`, `shadow` and `gradient` are absent unless some appearance sets them, and so is each part of `text`, so that whatever draws keeps reading the drawing defaults (ADR 0086), and what it drew before ADR 0088, where nothing sets them.
  - **Relations gain `opacity` only.** A relation's label has no text traits yet; A6.2 may add them, with a clarification here.
  - **A turn and a mirror are kept by moves.** `set-positions` takes a `PositionChange`: a position whose `rotation`, any number of degrees from −3,600 to 3,600, is stored from 0 up to 360, and whose `flip` is `horizontal`, `vertical`, `both` or `none`. Leaving either out keeps what is stored, so that layout, drags, alignment and resizing, which never name them, keep a shape turned; `0`, a whole turn or `none` takes it out. A placement that turns or flips a shape never joins the action before it (ADR 0065).
  - **The palette** is twelve colors in `core/src/palette.ts`: red `#E5484D`, orange `#E2600C`, amber `#B0820A`, green `#3D9A50`, teal `#0F9688`, cyan `#0789B5`, blue `#3E63DD`, purple `#9B4FC2`, magenta `#C2449E`, pink `#E5487F`, gray `#7D7D7D` and brown `#A87A52`, each at 3:1 or more on both canvases (blue's 3.38 on the dark canvas the least) and any two at least 26 apart in CIELAB, over the 20 the overlays ask for. A test holds both.
- 2026-11-02 (A6.1, Style, by a builder):
  - **The panel.** The inspector's Appearance section of one element, one relation, a note, an arrow or several items is the format panel: its heading keeps its name, then the scope choice, which applies to Style and Text, then a tablist of Style, Text and Arrange (one Tab stop, the arrow keys, Home and End). A tab with nothing to hold is not shown: a relation has Style and Arrange until A6.2 gives labels text traits, a note Style and Text, an arrow Style alone. The tab last chosen is kept for the tab's session and opens again for the next selection that has it.
  - **What moved into Text and Arrange.** Text holds an element's detail and badges, and where its text sits (`textPlacement`: its own, or its component's in the scope "Every <component>"); a note's text; for several elements, where their text sits. Arrange holds "In this view" (containers, stacking order, collapse), a relation's Route section, and for several items the stacking moves and the align, distribute and same-size actions of the canvas's menus. A6.2 and A6.3 add to them.
  - **The Style rows.** For an element: shape, fill, stroke, then gradient (no gradient, top to bottom or left to right, and its color; turned on, it runs to white, or to gray from a white fill), border width (0 to 16), border dash, corner radius (0 to 64), opacity in percent (20 to 100) and shadow (none or soft), then its image, part and face. An unset border width, corner radius or shadow says it comes "from the drawing defaults" and shows their value. For a relation: stroke, width (0.5 to 16), dash, opacity and automatic route. For several items: fill and stroke as before, then the elements' rows and the relations' line width, line dash and line opacity; a value they do not share shows as an empty box saying "Mixed" or a "Mixed" option, and a change is one batch, "Change the style of N shapes" or "… lines".
  - **"Set as the project's default"** writes, in one action, the gradient, border width, dash, corner radius, opacity and shadow an element sets itself into a project appearance of Essence, from which every component derives, so that every shape whose component sets none of them draws them; it is off, saying why, until the element sets one. Fill and stroke are left out: nearly every component sets its own, so a default would show almost nowhere. A relation's own stroke, width, dash, opacity and route go to a project appearance of "relates to" the same way. It is offered for one item, not for several.
  - **The swatches.** Every color field shows the palette's twelve swatches first, each named by its color ("Use Red, #E5484D"), then the recent and project colors as before. The app calls them swatches ("Swatches for fill"), since CONTEXT.md keeps "palette" alone for the component palette.
  - **The contrast hints of a border.** A border under 3:1 both on the canvas and on its own fill is pointed out for that canvas; a shape drawn with no border is said to blend in when its fill, and its gradient's color, are under 3:1 on the canvas, whatever its stroke. Never refused.
  - **What draws.** A shape in the scene carries `style`, only the traits some appearance sets that draw differently (a solid dash and an opacity of 1 are left out), so that a view whose appearances set none draws exactly as before; a test holds the sample's SVG the same with an empty appearance. A rectangle of either kind takes its own corner radius, up to half its shorter side (the drawing defaults' stays at most a quarter): above 0 it rounds a rectangle, 0 squares a rounded rectangle. A border of width 0 draws no line, nor the line a pixel wide that outlines solid borders; a dashed or dotted border is drawn in the lines' patterns along its outline and takes no such line either. Opacity fades a body's fill and border, its picture and its shadow, never its text; the plate a picture is drawn on stays whole. A shape's own soft shadow is drawn on any shape, a container too, its own "none" takes the drawing defaults' away, and own shadows are drawn in views of any size, since each is drawn once in its tile (the drawing defaults' still stop at 2,000 shapes). A gradient is a PixiJS `FillGradient` over the shape's bounds, made once for each pair of colors and direction, and on the dark canvas runs to its color adapted where stored colors are (ADR 0087); where the page has no 2D canvas the shape is filled in its fill. A shape with its own outline takes the border, dash, opacity and gradient, not the corner radius or the shadow. Far out, blocks show the opacity and the gradient only. A relation's opacity fades its line and arrowhead, never its label.
  - **The exports.** The SVG writes the same: `rx`, `stroke-width`, `stroke="none"`, `stroke-dasharray`, an `opacity` on a body's parts, a picture in a group of that opacity, a shadow's layers fainter, and a gradient as a `linearGradient` whose id names its two colors and its direction (`va-gradient-ffffff-3e63dd-right`), written once among the definitions, so that ids never collide; a faded relation is its line and arrowhead in a group of that opacity. The PDF and the comparisons are made from it, and the read-only page draws with the canvas.
  - **A container with a style of its own is drawn as its kind**, not as a quiet region, so that its style shows. Shapes that draw rows, and rows, keep their own drawing.

- 2026-11-02 (A6.2, Text, by a builder):
  - **What the text applies to.** A shape's `text` writes its element's name, and its alignment, padding, back and tag take the detail with it. It applies in full to a shape that writes its name inside its kind's outline (a box, an ellipse, a cylinder, a person, a picture's icon beside it) and to a name tag. A container's header and a shape with its own outline that writes its name inside it take the text color alone, for the name, with no back; far out, a container's name takes it too. Rows, tables, regions, faces, anchored parts and names in the band under a picture write as before. The relation's label still takes no text traits.
  - **The scene carries only what draws differently.** `ElementShape.text` holds the parts some appearance sets, without those that draw as nothing set does (an alignment in the middle, `position: inside`, wrapping, no italics, no underline), so that a view whose appearances set none, or set only those, draws exactly as before; tests hold the sample's SVG the same. Its colors, adapted on the dark canvas where the drawing defaults ask for it (ADR 0087), ride in `text.dark`, which the painted copy of a shape swaps in, as a gradient's color does.
  - **Fonts.** The name takes Roboto, the system's fonts or Roboto Mono, its size, and bold at 700 or regular at 400, the drawing defaults' weight otherwise. Italics are drawn in Roboto's loaded Italic and Bold Italic, a Medium name in italics in Bold Italic, since no Medium Italic is loaded; the system's fonts in the system's italic. Roboto Mono is loaded upright at 400 alone, so a name in it is written so whatever bold and italics say, and the panel says it: no weight or style the canvas has not loaded is ever asked for, and no slant is synthesized. The canvas installs a bitmap font for each weight, family and style the first time one is written (`labelFontOf`); the SVG writes `font-weight`, `font-style` and `font-family`, and the PDF embeds Roboto Italic, Bold Italic and Roboto Mono when they are used, as text emphasis does.
  - **Lines.** A styled name is broken into its lines once, in its own widths (`nameLinesOf`): wrapped at spaces to the column, a word too wide broken where it reaches it, at most the lines its block holds, the last ending in "…"; without wrapping, one line cut with "…", which the hover card then gives back. The canvas and the SVG write those lines as given, aligned left, centred or right in a column that is the label area less the padding at each side (the drawing defaults' side inset when the text sets none); the padding also keeps the block from the area's top or bottom. The detail aligns with the name. An underline is a line under each line of the name, as wide as it, a fourteenth of its size thick, drawn with the text and tinted with it on the canvas, a `rect` in the SVG.
  - **The text's back.** A background or a border draws a rectangle with corners of 3 behind the name and the detail, hugging the widest line, 4 units past it across and 2 down, in the background and lined a unit wide in the border; the name and detail then read on the background. A text color of its own wins; otherwise the text takes the color that reads on what it sits on.
  - **Name tags at a side.** `position` above, below, left or right writes the name and detail in a name tag attached there, centred along that side of the shape's outline or box, and nothing inside the shape. A tag above or below wraps as name tags do; one at the left or right at 160 units. The tag takes the text's font, size, weight, italics, wrapping and padding (8 and 4 units otherwise), and is filled in the background and lined in the border where they are set, the shape's fill and stroke otherwise. It is part of the shape exactly as name tags are: pointed at, selected, marked, related, routed around and laid out with it; layout now also makes room above a shape for a tag above it (`tagTop`, as `tagLeft`). A shape with no outline of its own attaches lines on its box and its tag together, as a drawing with parts does. A name that does not fit inside its own outline still goes to a tag below, or to the side its text names. Containers, rows, regions, anchored parts and shapes whose face writes the name hang none.
  - **The Text tab.** For one element, in the scope "Only this element" or "Every <component>": font, text size (8 to 48), weight, italics, underline, alignment, where its name is written (inside, or in a tag above, below, at the left or at the right), text color, text background and text border with the swatches first, long names (wrapped, or on one line cut with "…") and text padding (0 to 48), each saying where its value comes from ("from the drawing defaults", "reads on its fill" or "none" while unset), each change one action, sending that part alone; in the scope of the component, a part the element sets itself is taken back in the same action, its other parts kept. Then where its text sits, the detail and the badges, as before. For several elements, each part shows the value they share or "Mixed", and a change is one batch, "Change the text of N shapes". A text color under 4.5:1 on its back, or on its fill without one, is pointed out for each canvas it reads poorly on, adapted on the dark one where asked; never refused. A note keeps its text editor.

- 2026-11-02 (A6.3, Arrange, by a builder):
  - **How a shape turns.** Its position's `flip` mirrors it in its own axes, then `rotation` turns it clockwise, both about the middle of its rectangle (`canvas/turn.ts`). Its rectangle stays the level one, as stored; what it covers is the bounds of that rectangle turned. A shape with neither draws, and reads, exactly as before.
  - **What never turns.** No container turns, whether drawn as a region or as its kind, held open or collapsed: what it holds is laid out in its own axes and would not turn with it. Nor do rows, shapes that draw rows, and anchored parts. The scene leaves their `rotation` and `flip` out though the view stores them, and the Arrange tab turns their buttons and the degrees field off, saying why. This is wider than the Decision's "containers drawn as regions", for that reason.
  - **What turns, and what stays level.** The body, its own outline, its plate and picture, its solid's picture and its shadow are drawn through the turn, on the canvas, far out (a turned block is drawn at once) and in the SVG, where they sit in a `<g transform="matrix(...)">` inside the shape's group. The name and detail are never turned nor mirrored: inside the shape they are written in its level box, its rectangle, or its rectangle with width and height swapped about its middle when it is turned nearer a quarter turn than a half; a shape with its own outline writes in its outline's label area; a name tag hangs, level, from the side of the turned bounds; the marks for leaving relations and destinations stay level too. The PDF and comparisons follow the SVG, and the read-only page draws with the canvas.
  - **Pointing, marks and box selection.** A shape drawn in full holds a point by its level body, the point carried back through the turn; far out, by its turned box. The selection and hover marks run along the turned outline, and a dragged box must hold the turned bounds.
  - **Lines.** A turned shape is attached on its turned outline by the rules of ADR 0066, as a shape with its own outline is: its own outline, its plate, its ellipse or its box turned; a cylinder and a person on their box, as they are level. Automatic routes reach it by its turned bounds, and its lines are routed again when its turn or mirroring changes.
  - **Layout** spaces a turned shape by its turned bounds, with its tag, as it spaces a shape by its tag (`tagReachOf`, `tagLeft`, `tagTop`).
  - **Resizing.** A turned shape shows no resize handles, since they would pull its level sides from the turned ones; mirroring alone keeps them. The Arrange tab's width and height size it in its own axes.
  - **The Arrange tab** begins with, for one shape or several: Width and Height, in canvas units, with "Keep proportions" (on at first for shapes whose geometry keeps its proportions); From the left and From the top, from the container's shape or the canvas; Turn (degrees), "Turn 90°", and Mirror horizontally and Mirror vertically as toggle buttons; Pinned as a toggle button, saying what layout does. Then "In this view" for one element, and the stacking, align, distribute and same-size actions for several, as before. Each change is one batch of one `set-positions`, named "Resize", "Place", "Turn", "Mirror", "Pin" or "Unpin" with the shape's name or their count, every position in it pinned but for Unpin, which keeps the place; the stored sizes are kept unless the change sets them. A field set for several shapes sets each of them to that value; a value they do not share shows as an empty box saying "Mixed", or a toggle pressed "mixed". Rows and anchored parts take no part. The container a shape sits in is changed, for one element, by "In this view"'s Put inside, Move to and Take out, as before.
