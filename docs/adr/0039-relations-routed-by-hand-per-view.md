# Relations routed by hand, per view, as in draw.io

A view can route any relation it draws as a line by hand, and keeps only what differs from the automatic route in an optional `routes` map of its own file, keyed by relation id. For each end, a route may attach the line at one of the end's connection points, by name, or at a fixed place on its border, `{ side, at }` with `at` from 0 to 1 along the side, as draw.io's fixed points and as connection points already are; an end it leaves out attaches automatically. It may hold up to 64 bend points, in canvas units relative to the shape of the container named beside them: the nearest container that held both ends' shapes when they were set, a shape counting as holding itself, or the canvas when that was World. Its style is orthogonal, the default and stored as nothing, straight or curved. Two commands change routes, each one undoable action labelled by author: `set-route`, which changes only what it names and keeps only what differs from automatic, and `reset-route`, for one relation or several; Claude has them as `set_route` and `reset_route`. The core keeps routes true to the model: deleting a relation, directly or through an end, takes its routes in every view, as the delete preview lists; deleting the container of bend points takes the bend points; changing an end of a relation sends that end back to automatic and keeps the bend points while their container still holds both ends; a connection point a route names that leaves the end's appearance is replaced, in the same action, by the point now at the same side and place or by a border attachment there, so the line does not move. Bend points whose container no longer holds both ends, and attachments that no longer fit, such as one on an element that has become a row, are kept and ignored, and come back with the containment, as positions do (ADR 0019). An end on a relation, on World, or on an element drawn inside a row takes no attachment; one on a row or a shape that draws rows attaches only beside it, on the left or right. A relation is routed once per view, along the line between its ends' first shapes; an aggregated relation takes no route. Layout writes no route: ELK takes hand-chosen ends as fixed ports and is given no bend points, and its own route is used only for relations the view routes automatically. Checking a project only warns about a route that names an unknown relation, connection point or container, or attaches an end that never can be. The canvas draws hand routes through their bend points, curved ones as cubic Bézier spans flattened for hit testing, and edits them with handles at the ends, the bend points and the middle of each segment. We chose this because a route, like a position, is a drawing choice each view makes for itself (ADR 0009) and stays out of the model (ADR 0022); because relative bend points keep a route with what it runs between when a container moves; because storing their container, rather than recomputing it, never lets a change of containment reinterpret them against another shape; and because the core, not the canvas, keeps the rule that nothing names what is gone, so the app, Claude and an undo agree.

## Considered options

- **The route stored on the relation, in the model**: rejected. Each view draws a relation in its own place, and ADR 0022 keeps drawing choices out of the model.
- **Bend points in absolute canvas units**: rejected. Moving a container would leave every route inside it behind.
- **Bend points relative to the `from` end's shape**: rejected. Moving either end alone would drag the whole route with it or leave it, depending on which end it is.
- **The container worked out again on every read**: rejected. A change of containment, or of the view's nesting relation types, would silently move every bend point to another shape.
- **Dropping bend points when their container stops holding both ends**: rejected. A drag into a container and its undo, or a view nesting by another relation type for a while, would lose the person's work, which positions keep.
- **Removing an attachment when its connection point is removed**: rejected. The line would jump to an automatic place; keeping it where it was, by the point at the same place or at a border point there, is what the person last saw.
- **A per-end "automatic" value, and `"orthogonal"` written out**: rejected. Two spellings of the same route would differ in diffs; absence is the one way to say automatic.
- **An array of routes with a relation field, as positions are**: rejected. A map keyed by relation id cannot hold two routes for one relation, and its sorted keys diff cleanly.
- **Bend points given to ELK as constraints**: rejected for v0.1. ELK's layered algorithm does not keep given bend points; layout keeps hand routes by not touching them.
- **Curved routes drawn by layout**: left for later; v0.1's curves are chosen by hand.

## Consequences

- Builds older than this refuse view files that hold `routes`; the format stays at version 1, every new key being optional. The live protocol's operations can change a view's routes, so its version goes up with this change.
- The spec's "Curved routes (after v0.1)" now means curves drawn by layout; routes curved by hand are in v0.1.
- The core settles routes after every command, and after each command inside a batch, so a batch that reconnects a relation and then attaches its new end keeps the attachment. It costs nothing while no view keeps routes, and a scan of the routes when a command changes the model or the definitions.
- After layout, hand routes stay as they were; Reset route on the selection is how relations go back to the routes layout gives.
- The delete preview gains the routes a deletion removes or loses bend points from.

## Clarifications

- 2026-10-24: in an orthogonal route every bend point is a corner. From each point the route runs along its current axis to the next point's coordinate and turns there, so two points that share an x or a y are joined by one segment; it enters an end attached by hand along that end's stub, through a Z when the axis it arrives on would otherwise run back over the stub. So the corners of a route drawn by moving a segment, taken as bend points, give back that same route.
- 2026-10-24: connection points apply on the canvas to every line, not only to routes chosen by hand. An end attached automatically to a shape whose appearance has connection points attaches at the one facing its neighbour (§3.5 of `components.md`), with a stub, and lines between the same two ends attach 12 units apart along its side. Shapes keep their connection points, names included, so that the canvas snaps to them by name.
- 2026-10-24: a dragged end let go on the relation's other end is refused, as is one that would make containment form a cycle; the caption says which before release. With Alt, an end attaches at the exact border point nearest the pointer even deep inside its shape, so Alt never attaches automatically.
- 2026-10-24: while a route is dragged or edited from the keys, its line leaves its tiles and is drawn above the scene; once let go, it stays drawn as dropped until the change comes back, as dropped shapes do, so the old route never shows in between.
- 2026-10-24: a solid curve is cut for its tiles into pieces of its spans, each at most about 128 units of control polygon long, so that the frames' budget, which counts pieces, spreads their first drawing, and in the cell of its middle, and stroked with Bézier curves to within 0.1 units; a dashed or dotted curve is drawn along its flattened route with the dash pattern.
- 2026-10-24: the relation's menu is a menu of its own, `canvas/routing/RouteMenu.tsx`, since `RelationMenu.tsx` is the menu that chooses a new relation's type. It opens with a secondary press on a line, and with Shift+F10 or the menu key while only relations are selected.
- 2026-10-24: in route editing from the keys, Delete or Backspace with no bend point chosen says so rather than deleting the relation, and A, Insert and the arrow keys say what to choose when the handle chosen takes none of them. Every other key that types waits for the mode to end; zooming and panning still work.
- 2026-10-24: the canvas never drew ELK's routes: layout gives back only places. So ELK's own route is dropped for every relation, and the layout input gives each end chosen by hand as a fixed port (`LayoutRelation.fromPort` and `toPort`).
- 2026-10-24: bend points snap to the grid once the grid exists (Track A3); until then they snap only to their neighbours' x and y.
- 2026-10-25 (W24): how bend points snap to the grid. While the grid shows and snapping is on, a dragged bend point, one added from the middle of a segment included, lands on a multiple of 8 canvas units from the corner of the shape its route's bend points are kept relative to, or from the canvas's origin when that is World, so that what is stored is a multiple of 8; a neighbour's x or y within reach still comes first, so that runs stay straight, and is kept as it is. A dragged segment of an orthogonal route runs along a grid line of the same frame. While the keys edit a route, Shift with an arrow key takes a bend point or a segment to the next grid line in that direction, and an arrow key alone still moves it by 1. Alt places freely, as it does for moves; ends keep attaching as before.

- 2026-10-26 (W49): **automatic routes go around the shapes in their way.** Dogfooding found the canvas's routes running straight through the shapes between their ends, so that in nested views lines crossed other shapes and their names. An automatic orthogonal line between two shapes now keeps its stand-in route when that crosses nothing, and is otherwise routed again around every other shape and the name plates of the containers it runs in, the name and detail at a container's top left, a clearance of 16 units away, inside the container that holds both its ends (`canvas/routing/avoid.ts`): first by moving the stand-in's middle run to the middle of the nearest free channel, and else by the shortest route with the fewest turns through an orthogonal visibility grid made of the edges of what is in its way, A* over its points and the way the route faces. It leaves and enters its ends' shapes where the stand-in does, through the middle of a side moved by its offset among the lines between the same two shapes, so that siblings keep lanes of their own. A line that other lines end on is routed so too when each of those runs from a plain shape, and they follow its middle. What this does not touch keeps its route as before: a route set by hand, whatever it sets, a curved or straight one, one to a connection point or beside a row, one that ends on another line, one between a container and what it holds, and one with more than 60 shapes around it, which keeps each line's cost bounded however large the view.
  - **Off the main thread, and incremental.** The canvas routes in a worker of its own (`avoid.worker.ts`), the lines nearest the middle of the screen first, about 20 ms of work a reply, and draws the routes as they come, at most every 120 ms after the first; until then a line keeps its stand-in route. Each route is kept for its line while the scene keeps the line, and routed again only when its ends move, which makes the scene a new line, or when a shape is moved, resized, renamed, added or taken away where it stands across the route, or stood beside it, within 24 units, meanwhile keeping the route it had; a container that holds an end counts by its name plate alone, a shape inside an end not at all, and a line that kept its stand-in route, too much being around it or no way around, is not tried again until its ends move (`useAvoidedRoutes.ts`). The main thread composes the scene with the routes known and sends the worker the shapes, read again only where they changed, and the lines that lack a route; a view shown again asks for what it still lacked. The canvas area composes them onto the scene it draws once a layout's move is drawn, so that the move runs with the stand-in routes and the routes come after it; the canvas's hit testing and its PNG read the routes drawn, and the panels the scene as built, whose shapes are the same. The SVG and PDF, from the app, the command line and Claude's tools, route every line at once; the read-only page routes a view at once when it has at most 400 lines, and leaves a larger one as built.
  - **Measured.** The canvas benchmark has the phases (`bench/avoid.ts`, `?only=avoid` alone): a pan while every route of the 10,000-element view comes in, against the same pan before; and one shape moved again and again, the routing's own work on the main thread timed apart from the frame drawn with the routes that came. In Node on the development machine, under load, the worker routes every line of that view in 1.7 to 2.9 s, about 5,800 of its 9,600 lines going around something; after one shape moves, the main thread spends 8 to 20 ms composing, half of it finding again which lines go around shapes, where building the scene itself takes 20 ms, and under 1 ms asking; the worker routes the 2 lines the move touched in 3 to 15 ms, and composing the routes that came takes about 2 ms. Panning and zooming compose nothing. The examples' Structure view and A command's path go from 35 and 15 lines crossing a shape to 2 and 0, the two left being lines that end on another line.
  - **Considered:** ELK's own routes, rejected, since layout runs on demand and its routes would be wrong after the first move; routing in the scene's build on the main thread, rejected, since a fresh build of the 10,000-element view would take a second more; routing in slices on the main thread, rejected, since each slice would take frames from panning; routing in the shell, so that every panel read the routes, rejected, since a layout's move and every panel would be drawn again as routes came; a hierarchical router across containers, left for later, since routing within the container that holds both ends covers the views found so far.
